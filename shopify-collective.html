Selling Where the Problem Starts: A Retail Channel on Shopify Collective

Truck caps and campers are what block the factory camera, so I built a drop-ship channel aimed at the stores where those truck owners already shop.

  • Company: GG Offroad (ggoffroad.com)
  • Role: Co-Founder, Ecommerce & Growth Lead
  • Timeline: Sep 2026 – present

At a Glance

Challenge
Get a niche, install-required kit sold in other stores with a two-person team: no sales hire, no retailer inventory, and no price conflict with our own site.
What I did
Launched a drop-ship channel on Shopify Collective, tiered retailers by how well our kit fits what they already sell, designed an AI prospecting workflow that drafted 83 tailored invites I reviewed and sent, plus a tool that keeps checking Collective for new retailers, and gave partners a self-serve resource kit and a minimum advertised price policy.
Results
9 of 83 invited retailers accepted. 9 orders and $1,159 in new wholesale revenue in five weeks (8% of store orders), all from products those partners had never carried, led by a dealer that went from 2 of our products to the full catalog.
Skills
Channel and partnership strategy, partner targeting, outreach copywriting, pricing policy, Shopify Collective, AI workflow design

Context

  • GG Offroad sells camera relocation kits for the 2024+ Tacoma and 2022+ Tundra. A truck cap or camper blocks the factory camera; the kit puts it back.
  • Our buyers already shop at overland and truck-accessory stores for the cap or rack that causes the problem. I wanted the kit sold there too.
  • Shopify Collective is Shopify's drop-ship network: other Shopify stores list a supplier's products and sell them, and the supplier ships each order direct. Retailers carry no stock and pay nothing up front.
  • It's two of us, so the channel had to run without a sales hire.

Targeting by Fit

The starting question was simple: does this store's customer own the truck, or the gear, that breaks the camera? From there, I tiered every store by how well our kit fits what it already sells:

  • Tier 1: Toyota accessory and camera accessory stores. Their shoppers own the trucks we fit and are already buying camera gear, so our kit sits naturally in their catalog. The existing dealer that expanded to our full catalog is a Tier 1 store.
  • Tier 2: installer shops that fit toppers and other accessories that block the camera. Every install they do creates a customer for our kit, and the invite pairs our brake light cable for shell installs.
  • Tier 3: general off-road stores that sell truck accessories. Some overlap, more support risk. A short invite, sent only after Tiers 1 and 2.

Skipped on purpose: Jeep, van, UTV and apparel stores, which were over half of the keyword results.

A Pitch Written for Each Store

Collective caps invites at 500 characters, link included. Every invite named the retailer's own product in the second sentence, so it read as written for them, because it was:

Hi, Katie from GG Offroad. We make plug-and-play camera relocation kits for the 2024+ Tacoma and 2022+ Tundra. [Their product] covers the tailgate camera and blanks the digital mirror. Our kit moves the OEM camera to the shell, no splicing. Cheat sheets and install guides: [link] Price list attached.

  • Partners can sell without calling us. A reseller resource folder with cheat sheets, install guides and photos lets a store's staff answer fitment questions on their own.
  • Our own pricing stays protected. Every prospect gets a reseller toolkit (product information and installation documents) and our minimum advertised price policy, which keeps partners' advertised prices, including in their own Google Shopping listings, from undercutting ggoffroad.com.

The Prospecting System

I designed the workflow and Claude ran it; I approved and sent every invite myself. What I estimate would have been a 40-hour project took about 5 hours of my time.

  • Search: Claude worked through Collective's retailer directory with 20 search terms (Tacoma, topper, bed rack, upfitter and more) and checked each of our verified installers by name.
  • Sort: each store was checked against its own website, then tiered or skipped.
  • Draft: one invite per store, naming what that store sells, ready for my review.
  • Keep watching: a scheduled tool keeps checking Collective for new retailers, rerunning every search on the 1st of each month and skipping stores already contacted, so the pipeline refills without me. Its first two runs surfaced 15, then 8, new qualified retailers, all included in the 83 invites.

Results

  • 9 of 83Invited retailers accepted, an 11% acceptance rate
  • $1,159New wholesale revenue from 9 retailer orders in five weeks, all on products those partners hadn't carried before
  • 8%Of store orders (9 of 118), about 5% of net sales
  • ~40 → ~5Estimated hours of my time, with Claude running the prospecting

The honest read: all 9 orders so far came from two accepted retailers that already knew us, an existing dealer and a verified installer. But every dollar was new: the dealer had carried only 2 of our products and now lists the full catalog, because Collective removed the ordering friction. Moving both onto Collective also took manual invoicing off our plate. The other 7 accepted retailers haven't ordered yet, five weeks in, so converting them is the next test.

Why It Matters for Ecommerce

Complementary-product partners reach buyers at the moment of need, for a retail margin instead of ad spend. The work is choosing the right partners, making them self-sufficient, and protecting your own price.

What I Learned

  • Removing friction grows existing accounts. Making ordering easy took a 2-product dealer to our full catalog.
  • One qualifying question beats a long rubric. "Does this store's customer own the truck, or the gear, that breaks the camera?" sorted hundreds of results in one pass.
  • Make it easy for the retailer's staff. A store that can answer fitment questions without us is what makes a drop-ship partner sustainable.

What's Next

  • Get the other 7 accepted retailers to a first order, and follow up with Tier 1 and Tier 2 retailers who haven't accepted
  • Invite the remaining dealers and installers who still order by invoice
  • Track acceptance rate and time to first order by tier, to decide whether Tier 3 is worth sending

Note: Orders and sales from Shopify, Sep 28 to Nov 3, 2026. Revenue is GG Offroad's wholesale earnings; retailers keep a 30% margin.
