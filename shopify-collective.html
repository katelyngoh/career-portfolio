Selling Where the Problem Starts: A Retail Channel on Shopify Collective

Truck caps and campers are what block the factory camera, so I built a drop-ship channel with the stores that sell them, in about 5 hours of my time instead of 40.

  • Company: GG Offroad (ggoffroad.com)
  • Role: Co-Founder, Ecommerce & Growth Lead
  • Timeline: Sep 2026 – present

At a Glance

Challenge
Get a niche, install-required kit sold in other stores with a two-person team: no sales hire, no retailer inventory, and no price conflict with our own site.
What I did
Launched a drop-ship channel on Shopify Collective, targeted retailers with one question, sent 83 tailored invites through an AI prospecting workflow I designed and approved, set up a tool that keeps checking Collective for new retailers, and gave partners a self-serve resource kit and a minimum advertised price policy.
Results
9 of 83 invited retailers accepted, 8 of them new to us. 9 orders and $1,159 in wholesale revenue in the first five weeks (8% of orders), led by an existing dealer that expanded from 2 of our products to the full catalog.
Skills
Channel and partnership strategy, partner targeting, outreach copywriting, pricing policy, Shopify Collective, AI workflow design

Context

  • GG Offroad sells camera relocation kits for the 2024+ Tacoma and 2022+ Tundra. A truck cap or camper blocks the factory camera; the kit puts it back.
  • Our buyers already shop at overland and truck-accessory stores for the cap or rack that causes the problem. I wanted the kit sold there too.
  • Shopify Collective is Shopify's drop-ship network: other Shopify stores list a supplier's products and sell them, and the supplier ships each order direct. Retailers carry no stock and pay nothing up front.
  • It's two of us, so the channel had to run without a sales hire.

Sell Where the Problem Starts

I ranked every store by one question: does it sell the thing that breaks the camera?

  • Tier 1: cap, camper, canopy and bed rack sellers, plus upfit and install shops. Their product blocks the camera, so every sale creates a buyer for ours. The invite names their product and pairs our brake light cable for shell installs.
  • Tier 2: Toyota-focused parts stores. Their customers own the trucks we fit. The invite ties our fitment to their catalog.
  • Tier 3: general overland stores. Some overlap, more support risk. A short invite, sent only after Tiers 1 and 2.
  • Our verified installers. They already trust the product, and keep their installer pricing for in-shop orders.

Skipped on purpose: Jeep, van, UTV and apparel stores, which were over half of the keyword results.

A Pitch Written for Each Store

Collective caps invites at 500 characters, link included. Every invite named the retailer's own product in the second sentence, so it read as written for them, because it was:

Hi, Katie from GG Offroad. We make plug-and-play camera relocation kits for the 2024+ Tacoma and 2022+ Tundra. [Their product] covers the tailgate camera and blanks the digital mirror. Our kit moves the OEM camera to the shell, no splicing. Cheat sheets and install guides: [link] Price list attached.

  • Partners can sell without calling us. A reseller resource folder with cheat sheets, install guides and photos lets a store's staff answer fitment questions on their own.
  • Our own store stays protected. A minimum advertised price policy keeps partners from undercutting ggoffroad.com.

The Prospecting System

I designed the workflow and Claude ran it; I approved and sent every invite myself. What would have been a 40-hour project took about 5 hours of my time.

  • Search: Claude worked through Collective's retailer directory with 20 search terms (Tacoma, topper, bed rack, upfitter and more) and checked each of our verified installers by name.
  • Sort: each store was checked against its own website, then tiered or skipped.
  • Draft: one invite per store, naming what that store sells, ready for my review.
  • Keep watching: a scheduled tool keeps checking Collective for new retailers, rerunning every search on the 1st of each month and skipping stores already contacted, so the pipeline refills without me. Its first two runs surfaced 15, then 8, new qualified retailers.

Results

  • 9 of 83Invited retailers accepted (11%); 8 had never worked with us
  • $1,159Wholesale revenue from 9 retailer orders in the first five weeks
  • 8%Of store orders (9 of 118), about 5% of net sales
  • 40 → 5Hours of my time to build the channel, with Claude running the prospecting

The honest read: all 9 orders so far came from two partners who already knew us. An existing dealer that had carried 2 of our products switched to selling our full catalog once Collective made it easy, and a verified installer came on board too, which also took manual invoicing off our plate. Retailers keep a 30% margin, our standard wholesale rate. None of the newly accepted retailers has placed an order yet, five weeks in, so converting them is the next test.

Why It Matters for Ecommerce

Complementary-product partners reach buyers at the moment of need, for a retail margin instead of ad spend. The work is choosing the right partners, making them self-sufficient, and protecting your own price.

What I Learned

  • Warm partners convert first, and can grow. Moving an existing dealer onto Collective was the fastest revenue, and removing the ordering friction took them from 2 products to the full catalog.
  • One qualifying question beats a long rubric. "Does this store sell the thing that breaks the camera?" sorted hundreds of results faster than any scoring sheet.
  • Make it easy for the retailer's staff. A store that can answer fitment questions without us is what makes a drop-ship partner sustainable.

What's Next

  • Get the 9 accepted retailers to a first order, and follow up with Tier 1 retailers who haven't accepted, starting with cap and camper sellers
  • Invite the remaining dealers and installers who still order by invoice
  • Track acceptance rate and time to first order by tier, to decide whether Tier 3 is worth sending

Note: Orders and sales from Shopify, Sep 28 to Nov 3, 2026. Revenue is GG Offroad's wholesale earnings; retailers keep a 30% margin.
