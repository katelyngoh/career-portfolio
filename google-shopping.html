Launching Google Shopping from a Blocked Account

Every product was disapproved on day one. Five weeks later the full catalog was approved in 59 countries, at $0 ad spend, in about 5 hours of my time instead of 40.

  • Company: GG Offroad (ggoffroad.com)
  • Role: Co-Founder, Ecommerce & Growth Lead
  • Timeline: Sept – Nov 2026

At a Glance

Challenge
Search drove about a third of sales, but every product was blocked from Google Shopping. Get the catalog live internationally as a two-person, home-based brand with no ad budget.
What I did
Traced the account-level block to one root cause, split the flagship kits into truck-specific products to match how owners search, priced international shipping from real checkout quotes, and got the account reinstated after a misrepresentation flag.
Results
720 disapproved products to 0, and 1,380 product-country listings approved with 0 limited across 59 countries. Google clicks up 174% in the latest 28 days, at $0 ad spend.
Skills
Google Merchant Center, product feed optimization, search-intent merchandising, international ecommerce, Shopify, AI-assisted operations

Context

  • GG Offroad sells camera relocation kits for Toyota Tacoma and Tundra trucks. Search already drove about a third of sales, but none of the catalog appeared in Google Shopping, Google's free product listings.
  • I ran the project with Claude as an operations partner: auditing the feed, editing products through the Shopify API, and working through Merchant Center diagnostics with me. What would have been a 40-hour project took about 5 hours of my time.

Getting and Staying Approved

  • 720 products blocked, one root cause. Google had verified a different web address (our Shopify subdomain) than the one our product feed used. Fixing that single setting cleared every product.
  • Verified without a storefront. Passed Google's identity checks as a home-based business by keeping our legal name, address and contact details identical across the site, policies and Google, and dropped one market whose local registration requirement was holding every listing at "Limited."
  • Recovered from a misrepresentation flag in 2 days. Working fast with Claude meant a burst of correct changes landed during Google's initial review, which made the account look unstable. I audited every field Google cross-checks (business name, address, policies, shipping, promotion), fixed the mismatches, and froze edits until the review cleared. The account was reinstated two days later.

Rebuilding the Catalog Around How Owners Search

  • One product per truck. Owners search for their own truck, so a combined kit matched neither search well. I split the two flagship kits into Tacoma and Tundra products, with 301 redirects to keep existing links working. Early sign: two of the three GOOGLE10 orders so far came through the new Tundra page.
  • Rewrote titles to lead with year and model ("2024+ Toyota Tacoma Digital Rearview Mirror…") and descriptions to cover every fitment term buyers use.
  • Cleaned up categories, structured attributes, and custom labels by truck and product type, including a hidden Google category field that was overriding the fix on five products.

Shipping Costs That Match Checkout

  • Google blocked 233 products in 32 countries because shipping showed in US dollars next to local-currency prices. Every one of our 59 countries uses its own currency.
  • Priced from real quotes, not guesses. I ran test checkouts for real FedEx rates in each currency and set flat rates padded 20%, so a listing never shows less than checkout will charge, the kind of mismatch that triggers misrepresentation reviews. International buyers still check out in their own currency with duties prepaid.
  • A promotion that pays for itself. Rejected a free-shipping offer after realizing it only changed what customers paid on one low-priced item, and launched a trackable GOOGLE10 code instead.

Results

  • 720 → 0Disapproved products, traced to one root cause
  • 1,380Product-country listings approved across 59 countries, 0 limited
  • +174%Google clicks in Merchant Center: 728 in the latest 28 days
  • 40 → 5Hours of my time, with Claude as an operations partner

Why It Matters for Ecommerce

Free listings are the cheapest high-intent shelf a DTC brand has, but only for a feed Google trusts. Getting there took root-cause diagnosis and policy discipline, not a volume of edits.

What I Learned

  • AI speed needs pacing. Claude let me make changes faster than Google's review could absorb them, which is what triggered the misrepresentation flag. I now stage changes after approval rather than before.
  • Clicks aren't revenue yet. Google clicks rose 174%, but store sessions from Google held roughly flat, so I'm not claiming a revenue lift. Tying Shopping clicks to orders is the next phase.

What's Next

  • Add installed-on-truck and mirror-view photos, the biggest remaining listing-quality gap
  • Group reviews across the split products so star ratings show on every listing
  • Test a small Performance Max campaign, segmented by the truck and product-type labels already in the feed

Note: Click data is from Google Merchant Center (latest 28 days vs the 28 days before); session data is from Shopify Analytics. All listings were free; no ad spend.
